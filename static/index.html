The design thesis

A late task is not always a late project.

The risk we demonstrate is a hidden threshold: a task can consume nearly all its float before the project finish moves. The tool makes that buffer visible and lets a project lead test the next step.

Research hypothesis for a possible Prestige discussion—not a claim about Prestige's internal processes or a replacement for Autodesk, Primavera or project-controls staff.
The problem we are testing

When does a delay stop being “absorbed” and start threatening handover?

Example: procurement has 15 days of total float. A 14-day slip does not move modeled handover, but leaves only one day of buffer. At 15 days, that path becomes critical; one more day pushes handover by one day in this simplified network.

      How this could fit a real project stack

      05 / ARCHITECTURE
      1. Ingest (future adapter): milestone dates and activity links from an approved schedule export; issue and procurement status from allowed data sources.
      2. Normalize: align IDs, calendars, owners, status timestamps and dependencies; quarantine stale or incomplete records.
      3. Simulate: propagate a disruption through the dependency graph and compare the original plan against plausible interventions.
      4. Explain: show the critical chain, affected successors, estimated recovery, assumed cost and reasons behind the ranking.
      5. Govern: project owner reviews the evidence and action. Log assumptions, version and approval; never auto-approve construction or regulatory decisions.

      Model card & guardrails

      06 / TRUST
      • Method: deterministic dependency graph + longest-path schedule propagation; a separate experimental logistic-regression model uses synthetic labels only.
      • Not claimed: no real-world risk calibration, no causal proof, no Autodesk / Primavera connection and no access to Prestige data.
      • Needed for validation: approved activity histories, working calendars, resource constraints, intervention cost ranges and actual planned/actual completion outcomes.
      • Before a pilot: test against historic projects, report error and sensitivity, review false alerts, verify security / access control, and retain human sign-off.
